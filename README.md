# Meadow Goat

[![CI](https://github.com/ripopov/goat-craft/actions/workflows/ci.yml/badge.svg)](https://github.com/ripopov/goat-craft/actions/workflows/ci.yml)
[![Deploy to GitHub Pages](https://github.com/ripopov/goat-craft/actions/workflows/pages.yml/badge.svg)](https://github.com/ripopov/goat-craft/actions/workflows/pages.yml)

**Play online: [ripopov.github.io/goat-craft](https://ripopov.github.io/goat-craft/)**

A blocky meadow surrounded by an endless ocean, with a procedural goat, winding rivers, ponds full of fish, a wandering herd of animals, a three-minute day/night cycle, and growing enemy waves that attack a living Hearth Tree, built with three.js.

The outer shoreline slopes into deep ocean by **±360 world units on either X/Z axis**, measured from the center. Ocean continues forever beyond that boundary. A warning appears offshore; swimming past **±390** awakens Cthulhu for a cinematic emergence, tentacle grab and fatal plunge. Gameplay pauses during the cutscene, and a fresh game starts **five seconds after death**. Reduced-motion mode removes camera shake.


## Run it

Open **`index.html`** directly in a browser (`file://`). No server, build step or package install is required for local play. Keep the folder structure intact.

The modules are **classic scripts** (IIFEs) sharing a small `window.Meadow` namespace. They are deliberately not ES modules: browsers block `import` and `fetch` from `file://` for security reasons, which would require a local server. Classic `<script src>` tags work from disk, so this remains a zero-server static page. See [the architecture and extension guide](docs/architecture.md) for module responsibilities, dependency order, and adding species or special waves.

## Controls

- **WASD / arrow keys** — move
- **Shift** — gallop (2× speed, four-beat gait), or swim faster in water
- **Space** — jump (up to three times in the air); hold to swim toward the surface. Near shore, jump toward a surface block to climb out, or keep moving into the bank for an automatic shore jump.
- **Ctrl** — hold to dive deeper; WASD still works while Ctrl is held
- **W / S + mouse look** — swim along the view pitch; A / D strafe horizontally. Space / Ctrl override vertical steering.
- **Left click / tap / E** — mine the block under the center reticle (up to 5.5 units away, aimed in any direction from the camera), or place the selected block on the aimed face instead. Attacks are never bound to input here: the goat fights automatically. P gives an unobstructed first-person view for precise mining.
- **Right click / R** — on the ground, break the block beneath the striking hoof or place the selected block. At any point in a jump, press to use **Aerial Stomp**: dive hooves-first and release a shockwave on landing. Damage and radius grow from **zero** with the distance fallen after the press: roughly **5× normal headbutt damage** from a single-jump apex and **20×** from a triple-jump apex, including Nova Core upgrades. The radius grows to about **8 world units** for a full single-jump drop and **14 units** for a triple-jump drop. A gold ring marks the actual damage radius; walls block the hit. Works with any selected item on every jump, triggers on press even while moving the mouse or holding the left button, and cancels on entering water. Longer falls also make the shockwave brighter and thicker. Three impact sounds distinguish light hoof clacks, medium stone cracks, and heavy booms with falling rubble.
- **Q / Upgrades** — open training; selects a visible animal within 5.5 units, otherwise your goat. Switch to your goat using its tab. Q toggles the menu open and closed. The world pauses while training.
- **1–9 / 0** — select hotbar slot (slot 1 is horns and hooves; broken blocks stack in the other slots)
- Selected blocks ride on the goat's horns. A hollow mint contour previews placement; coral means the cell is blocked. A gold contour marks the aimed mining block, and a gold animal outline shows which NPC Q will open. Enemies are never outlined for combat: attacks are automatic.
- **P** — toggle between third-person and first-person (FPS) cameras
- **T / Strategic view** — enter or leave the live RTS camera. Your goat autonomously defends the Hearth and collects coins while the battle continues. Your previous FPS / third-person view returns when you leave.
- In strategic view: **WASD / arrows / left drag** pan relative to the screen, **Shift** pans faster, **scroll** zooms, **Z / X** or the arrow buttons rotate the map in quarter turns, and **right-drag** rotates freely. The compass resets north up. **Space / Hearth** centers the objective, and **Goat** centers on your goat. Select a cannon in the workshop or use **Shift+1–0**, then click dry, supported ground. A green tower ghost and ring show a valid site and attack range; red means blocked or unaffordable. Click repeatedly to deploy more of the selected tower. **Right-click** cancels selection; **T / Close** returns to your goat.
- Click a deployed tower to inspect its stats and kills, or **Pack tower**. You can also mine any tower block using your horns in FPS / third-person view. The whole cannon becomes one persistent item: walk your goat over it to collect it into the hotbar. Select that stack and place it like a block, or redeploy it from the RTS workshop at no resource cost. Full inventories leave the pickup on the ground. Towers also pack if their foundation is mined, and enemies can chew them when breaching defenses.
- **Mouse look** (button / **F**) — capture the pointer and steer either camera with the mouse; **F** releases it. Once enabled, capture returns automatically after closing training, returning from strategic view, or refocusing the game, until you explicitly release it. Use **Q** to close training, **T** to leave strategic view, and **/** to close the console.
- **Drag / pinch** — orbit and zoom while the pointer is free (drag and pinch are the touch fallback)
- **Say hi** — bleat and jump
- **Music** (top-right button) — toggle the original Iron Canopy melodic soundtrack; enabled by default and starts with the first click or keypress permitted by the browser
- **Sound** (top-right button) — mute/unmute effects independently of music; enabled by default
- **? Controls** (bottom-left) — toggle the compact controls guide; click the button again to close it
- **/ — Console** — open a developer console; **/** again closes it, mirroring how Q toggles the upgrades menu and T toggles the strategic view. `money` grants 10,000 coins and 10,000 of every tower resource; `spawn` starts the next enemy wave immediately; `spawn 10` jumps directly to wave 10 (and lower levels work too). Enter runs a command, and Up / Down recalls history.

### Cannon workshop

While strategic view is active, your goat prioritizes enemies attacking the Hearth, then other threats within 45 units of it. With no nearby threats it collects reachable coins anywhere on the map, without a distance cap, then returns to a clear guard position around the tree. It runs, aims and uses its normal automatic headbutts, stomps and trained elemental attacks. Routes avoid deep water and the void, detour around walls and roots, jump one-block rises, and descend from roofs or the Hearth canopy onto checked dry land using normal gravity. Cave stairs work beneath low ceilings, and long return trips continue across bounded path searches. If you enter strategic view while swimming, it seeks a nearby bank and climbs ashore. Construction invalidates routes; unreachable or stalled targets are temporarily skipped. If no existing exit is reachable from a cave or deep hole, the goat digs a staircase through natural blocks, clearing headroom, retaining each supporting step, and jumping up toward the surrounding terrain. Escape mining creates normal resource drops and ignores the selected hotbar item; it protects player construction and its immediate foundations, the Hearth, and permanent roads/gates. Leaving strategic view cancels queued excavation immediately. A progress watchdog also covers missing routes and swimming: after 10 seconds without movement or successful excavation, or 30 seconds without progress toward its destination, a trapped goat is rescued to a checked, dry location near the Hearth. Rescue preserves inventory and upgrades, cancels pending impacts, and never mines defenses. Resting guards and active combat do not trigger rescue; if no safe rescue destination exists, it waits and retries. Returning to direct view immediately restores your controls. The pilot does not spend resources, mine through defenses, or grant extra combat strength; towers and training still matter.

The strategic HUD keeps a tower strip and all ten inventory slots visible beneath the animal upgrade toolbar. Use **1–0** or click a slot to equip an inventory item, then click the map to place blocks against the aimed face, redeploy packed towers, or mine with Horns & Hooves. Inventory actions work at command-camera distance and retain shared terrain, protected-block and actor collision rules. Blocks and packed towers stay selected while their stack has items, so each click places another. Empty stacks return to inspection; tower blueprints stay selected until canceled. Use **I** to move bag items into the hotbar. **Inspect / cancel** or right-click returns to tower inspection. On narrow screens, swipe or scroll the tower strip horizontally to reach all ten towers; **Shift+1–0** selects their blueprints. Tower stats and packing controls appear only when a blueprint or deployed tower is selected.

Towers occupy one grid cell and are one to three blocks tall. Build within 220 meters of the Hearth, on solid, dry ground with clearance for the whole tower. Construction checks allies, enemies, the Hearth and every occupied cell before spending anything. Up to 80 towers can be deployed; their collision participates in the same enemy navigation and breach system as walls. Towers keep firing in both camera modes. Walls and terrain block shots and splash propagation.

Higher foundations extend tower range by **20% of base range per 2 Y**, beginning at **Y 16**: Y 14 and below use base range, Y 16 gives +20%, Y 18 gives +40%, and Y 20 gives +60%. This applies to every tower, including builder Frost Spires. The placement ring and selected-tower stats show the effective range and elevation bonus. The ranges below are base values.

The five premium towers (Ember, Storm, Watchman, Arcane and Sunspire) cost 50% more and deal 50% more direct damage than their original blueprints; whole-coin and whole-block prices round up. Ember burn also gains 50% damage. Wood uses oak logs; soil uses dirt; ores use their mined block stacks. Packed tower items are consumed before fresh construction costs. Attacks prioritize enemies nearest the Hearth; slows last 2.5 seconds, fire deals 0.75 damage/second and poison 0.7 damage/second for four seconds. Reapplying a type refreshes its effect instead of stacking it indefinitely. Kills leave collectible coins. Every shot deals damage only when its projectile collides with a live enemy or gate. Towers prioritize enemies before gates. Physical shots lead walking targets and can miss; elemental homing shots follow their target. Fast shots use swept collision so they cannot pass through a body between simulation ticks. Walls built during flight block attacks. Splash victims are checked at impact: Venom poisons enemies within 2.4m, refreshing one four-second poison per victim. Arcane slashes hit up to two additional enemies within 2.8m of the struck victim for half damage, nearest first, with a sweeping violet crescent. Walls block splash, slashes and chain links. Lightning jumps travel and hit in sequence; Watchman pierces up to two bodies and Sunspire up to three, both in flight order. Fired projectiles keep flying if their tower is packed. Reduced motion keeps the same visible projectiles and combat timing while suppressing recoil, spinning and decorative bursts. Projectiles and effects have separate budgets; shared geometry and materials are reused.


Each cannon has its own architecture: timber bow limbs and strings, an open braced Watchman frame with a ladder and scope, armored cannon bores, a crown of ice, a vine-covered fanged idol, copper coil windings, a stone siege cradle, an orbiting arcane armillary, and a gilded sun wheel. Staggered masonry, buttresses, inset runes, rivets and distinct stone, timber and metal palettes keep them readable from the strategic camera. Model geometry and materials are baked and shared; firing mechanisms animate independently on each deployed tower.

Ballista, Mortar, Watchman and Earthshaker towers carry warm voxel torches; Ember's braziers burn with matching flames. Frost crystals, Venom runes, Storm coils, Arcane prisms and Sunspire crowns cast soft light in their own colors after dark. Lighting fades with the day/night cycle. Up to eight nearby visible towers share shadow-free lights, with smooth handoffs as the camera moves; packed items and placement previews emit no light. Reduced motion keeps the flames and lighting steady.

Flying projectiles also illuminate nearby terrain and enemies: amber bolts, orange mortar shells, icy cyan shards, red-orange embers, green venom, blue lightning, pale-gold sniper darts, ochre boulders, violet arcane shots and golden sun lances. Four additional shadow-free lights follow nearby visible shots, including arcing, homing and chained flights. Their glow is subdued in daylight and clears on impact or expiry; packing a tower preserves the light of its shots already in flight.

Soft glowing sparks and expanding smoke use two fixed GPU particle batches (900 glow slots and 220 smoke slots), with reusable buffers and shared shaders. Trails sample the actual traveled segment, ballistic models follow their flight tangent, and temporary lightning geometry is released when its afterimage fades. Burn, poison and frost particles follow affected enemies. Reduced motion suppresses decorative particles and moving mechanisms while preserving combat timing and visible projectiles. Everything is generated locally with no texture downloads or extra dependencies.

The meadow has **winding rivers, coastal seas and blocky ponds**. Rivers run continuously across chunk boundaries and meet the seas at a common waterline. Terraced banks and shallow coastal shelves descend into sea beds more than ten world units deep. A river lies about 56 world units along +Z from the tree; seas lie farther east and west. A nearby pond sits west of the starting clearing at **(-54, 6)**, and other ponds are seeded deterministically on a 64-block lattice, away from the clearing, channels and coasts. Ponds have raised grassy banks, muddy swimming basins, transparent animated water, lily pads, and five to nine fish. Fish swim in stable elliptical shoals, wag their tails, and produce small surface ripples. The herd prefers to stay on dry ground.

The goat wades in shallows and automatically **swims when its body is immersed**. Swimming uses buoyancy and water drag instead of walking on the bed, with a smoothly blended, alternating four-leg paddle, body roll, and a raised head. Hold Space to rise or Ctrl to dive; a surface swimmer can scramble over a one-block bank. Swimming resets air jumps without consuming them. Mouse look, free-pointer dragging and zoom all work underwater, and forward/backward swimming follows the camera pitch. The camera follows dives, retracts around solid banks and blocks, and gains blue distance fog underwater. Wakes, rising bubbles and synthesized splashes provide water feedback. Water now uses a **local voxel fluid simulation**. Natural ponds, rivers and seas supply renewable source cells. Breaking a bank lets water leak through the opening, fall down shafts and spread into lower ground or mined tunnels. Flow becomes shallower with distance; a full supported flow spreads up to seven cells. Solid blocks contain and displace it, and disconnected streams drain after the opening is sealed. Water never climbs uphill. Source-to-source conversion, buckets, lava and water currents are not implemented.

Collision checks the occupied voxels rather than treating everything below the terrain surface as solid. The goat can walk through mined tunnels, hits low ceilings when jumping, slides along walls, and lands on block ledges. The goat, herd and zombies share upright body boxes that keep their size when the model turns; horns, noses and animation poses do not enlarge collision. Movement follows player input or the NPC target independently of visual facing. Contact resolves at the block face, preserving wall slides and allowing characters to walk away from inside corners. True overlaps recover locally; touching a wall does not trigger a backward nudge. Herd spacing accounts for height, so an animal above a bridge cannot obstruct one below it. Dry bridges remain walkable even when water flows underneath them. Placement checks the goat, herd and zombies before consuming a block. Underground cameras use the actual floor below them. Pickups fall when their support is mined and cannot be collected through walls. Grass and flowers disappear when their soil is mined or covered.

Mining through the final terrain layer opens into the void. After falling **three blocks (6 world units)** below the world bottom, the goat returns to the sky **six blocks above the Hearth Tree crown**, then free-falls onto its leaves. The camera follows the return immediately; inventory, coins and training are preserved. Pending mining and attack poses clear before the new fall.

Living enemies that fall three blocks below the world return under parachutes, normally **24–32 meters (12–16 blocks) from the Hearth**. If that band has no safe landing, they drop farther out, up to 48 meters away. They appear 16 meters above a dry, clear landing site, descend at 3.8 meters per second with a lavender ground warning, and recover for 0.9 seconds before advancing. They remain vulnerable during the drop and retain their species, wounds and wave stats; returning grants no loot or extra wave member. A mined-out landing zone lets them fall through and return again.

Gameplay runs at a fixed 60 simulation steps per second, with bounded catch-up after slow frames and no hidden-tab simulation. Block edits update collision immediately and batch mesh rebuilding by chunk. Prop meshes omit hidden faces, construction looks up placed blocks by chunk, and chunk streaming reuses its nearby-first queue. Fluid updates process at most 1,024 queued cells per tick; settled water needs no simulation work. Generation caches are bounded, and flowing cells outside streamed chunks are reconstructed from saved terrain edits when the area returns.

Deployed towers share instanced GPU batches by geometry and material, including shadow rendering, while retaining independent aiming, recoil and loading mechanisms. Projectile collision uses a six-meter spatial grid rebuilt from current enemy positions each simulation tick; swept hits, splash and chain attacks query nearby cells. Enemy voxel transforms and palette colors are precomputed. Catch-up steps and multi-victim spells prepare the horde only once for the displayed frame, and instance uploads cover only occupied slots.

Run `node scripts/check-performance.mjs` for a seeded, sustained battle at both caps (200 enemies and 80 mixed towers). It reports median and p95 CPU times plus visible mesh and shadow batch counts; timings exclude WebGL and depend on hardware. An optional frame count, such as `node scripts/check-performance.mjs 1200`, extends the measured run after a 120-step warmup. Add a blueprint ID, such as `node scripts/check-performance.mjs 360 arcane`, to stress 80 rapid-fire towers. Combatants remain invulnerable so the workload cannot shrink as enemies die.

The player starts at **(8, 24)** in an open, level meadow, with the ancient **Hearth Tree** at its center, world coordinates **(0, 0)**. The meadow stays flat and dry for a **15-block radius** (30 world units) around the tree, leaving room to build defenses in every direction. Natural trees, rocks, ponds and rivers stay outside this space. A five-block terrain transition leads into denser woodland, hills and the infinite procedural wilderness. The builders place defensive walls on the lawn at least ten blocks from the tree and quarry turf outside the clearing; players can still dig and build everywhere on the lawn.

The tree's half-unit voxels form a forked oak, a layered green-and-gold crown, and mossy roots fitted to the terrain. An inset amber heart, golden sap seams, hanging lanterns, and slowly drifting embers make it a warm landmark after dark. The trunk, roots, and canopy have persistent collision; the foundation is protected from mining and herd construction. The tree starts with **100 HP**, shown alongside the coin balance and living enemy count in the defense HUD. Its glow fades as its health falls.

After **30 seconds** of preparation, the first three waves send **6, 10, and 14 enemies**. Each wave adds four enemies, up to **200 per wave**. All-boat waves round up to complete four-enemy crews (64 on wave 15, 144 on wave 35, and 200 on wave 75). Enemies enter in **squads of three**, growing to four on wave four and five on wave nine. Squad members spawn 0.6 seconds apart at first, with a 4.2-second recovery gap between squads; later waves tighten these intervals to 0.3 and 2.4 seconds. The opening attacks from the goat's side of the meadow, wave two alternates squads between **two opposing approaches**, and wave six adds a **third front**. These changes fall between species introductions to avoid stacking difficulty spikes. The front rotates between waves. The HUD shows approach directions and the next squad countdown; **amber warning triangles on the minimap** mark incoming approaches, including during preparation.

Ground enemies emerge from **four destructible Oblivion gates** deep in the forest, roughly **75–89 world units** from the Hearth. Jagged obsidian horns, molten seams and animated red-orange portals mark each entrance; red gate symbols remain visible on the minimap. Towers damage gates with real projectile hits, splash, lightning, piercing shots and lingering burns or poison. World health bars, flashing stone, rippling portals and weapon impacts show damage. Mining cannot remove a gate frame. Each starting gate has **50× the first Boss's health (12,000 HP)**, locked for the run. Destroying it collapses its frame and opens exactly one replacement **50 voxel blocks (100 world units) farther from the Hearth**, along the same direction with grid snapping. The replacement has **100× Boss health (24,000 HP)**, a violet health bar and minimap marker. Destroying it seals that lane permanently. Gate foundations and winding, six-unit-wide stone roads remain as ruins; roads to the planned second line are graded at world creation, including raised crossings over water and stone embankments supporting dry approaches. The wider island leaves dry forest around all four replacement sites, with tree canopies kept clear of the roads and portals. Queued ground arrivals switch to surviving gates. After **all eight gates** are destroyed, the invasion gives way to **Cthulhu, the final boss**. Remaining enemies and boats vanish and waves stop. A giant jade elder rises beside the meadow, with articulated facial tentacles, claws, a horned crown and broad ribbed wings. He has **twice the health of a regular Boss at the current wave** (using wave 7 as the minimum), shown in a dedicated health bar. After a three-second awakening, he advances toward the Hearth; a mint-colored warning ring and raised tentacles precede his 4-HP strike, repeated every seven seconds. Normal melee, elemental attacks, tower projectiles, burns and slows work after emergence. He never disappears through the stalled-enemy watchdog. Defeat him to earn victory: the camera frames his six-second collapse and rising jade embers, then sweeps back to the Hearth for golden light, expanding rings and a victory fanfare. A keyboard-accessible **Defend again** button appears after ten seconds. Reduced-motion mode uses a stationary camera and calm effects. Strategic panning and terrain streaming cover the expanded siege area. Enemies favor the roads but can detour around defenses. The paving cannot be mined or destroyed; players can still build walls on top. Squads alternate between the announced gates. **Worms spawn underground beneath the announced gates**, tunnel toward the Hearth, then emerge in a 19–36-unit band and crawl on the surface. Parachute drops and sea boats retain their separate delivery routes. Blocked gate attempts retain the queued enemy and retry without releasing a catch-up burst. There are never more than **200 living enemies total**, across all species. Clear every enemy to earn a **6–24 coin bonus** and start a **10-second preparation break**. After every tenth wave (10, 20, 30, and so on), the break lasts **one full minute**. Unfinished waves never stack on top of each other.

All enemy base health and wave-level health gains are **4×** their original values. Opening zombies have **8 HP**; Stone brutes have **28 HP** before wave scaling. Health stays at its base value through **wave three**, letting the opening teach positioning and squad control before durability starts increasing. Health then grows toward **5&times;**, while speed grows more gradually toward **3&times;** its wave-one value. Enemy stats are fixed when they spawn. Each zombie follows the terrain toward the tree and eats **1 HP every 3 seconds** while in contact with its trunk or roots. At zero HP, attacks and spawning stop and **Defend again** restarts the game.

Zombies walk around high walls and hills, jump up **one block**, and cannot jump up two. All surface enemy species swim across deep water with buoyancy and paddling animations, then jump onto a clear bank up to one block above the waterline. NPCs that fall into water paddle back toward a shore and use the same exit check; normal wandering still avoids entering water. Shore jumps need a nearby solid landing and clear headroom. Shared navigation covers the defense arena and extends along the four forest approaches to the distant gates; it updates locally when blocks change. If no walking route exists, zombies plan a breach and chew each obstructing block for **10 seconds**. Terrain can be tunneled and player-built walls can be dismantled. Every chewed block becomes a **persistent collectible drop**; nearby matching materials stack and can be collected and placed again. The tree's protected foundation, hell gates and roads remain intact, and the arena stays loaded while the goat explores elsewhere.

With any hotbar item selected, the goat attacks **automatically**, Vampire-Survivors style, whenever an enemy is in reach: eight headbutts or four stomps kill an opening zombie, each headbutt hits only one enemy for 1 base damage, while stomps hit up to eight, and NPC hunters strike one target at a time; closest valid targets take priority. The goat never turns you toward a target: your facing stays fully mouse-controlled, bolts and fireballs auto-aim at the nearest enemy, melee headbutts swing at what you look at, and the stomp pounds everything in range all around. All attack ranges are **tripled**: the default headbutt reaches about 8.4 units and the stomp 9.3, so the goat clears a wide circle around your position while you build and mine. **Arc Antlers** training re-forges the horns of the player **and every trained hunter** into an elemental caster: tier 1 throws a lightning bolt at the nearest enemy within **9 blocks**, tier 2 chains electricity across up to three foes within **12 blocks**, and tier 3 lobs a fireball up to **30 blocks** that bursts with a **3-block blast radius**. Walls stop the bolt; a fireball instead bursts on the wall and burns whatever sits near the impact. Killed enemies leave **physical gold pickups**, including kills made by hunters. Walk close to attract and collect them, just like blocks; they never take a hotbar slot. Coin stacks persist and retain their full value. The wallet changes when coins are collected, rather than on a kill. Wave-clear bonuses are paid directly.

In **strategic view**, click Mallow, Clover, Bramble or Poppy's portrait in the workshop toolbar to open that animal's upgrades from anywhere on the map. The compact menu shows current and next effects, tier progress, owned resources and prices, with smaller icons and costs beside training buttons. Cards use two columns on desktop and one on phones; short screens scroll within the dialog while the close control stays accessible. Each animal keeps its own upgrades; a sheep's training never affects another sheep. Training lasts until the run ends. You start with **no coins or materials**, carrying only your built-in Horns & Hooves. Gather supplies and earn gold to build towers or train. In strategic view, workshop cards have a red border whenever you lack the resources to build them; packed towers remain available for free redeployment. Mine natural trees for more logs. **Stone is guaranteed beneath the grass and one dirt layer**; dig two blocks down, then mine the exposed stone. An enemy watchdog measures each enemy's closest approach to the Hearth, ignoring animation and tiny jitter. **After 30 simulation seconds without reducing that distance by at least half a world unit, the enemy explodes and drops its normal collectible coins.** Wall breaks, attacks, casting, circling and route replanning do not reset the timer. The four-unit blast destroys ordinary terrain and player-built blocks, leaving recoverable materials, but cannot destroy the gates, road paving, protected foundation or Hearth tree. It does not damage the objective or other actors. The watchdog applies to every species and frees live slots immediately; its effects are bounded and respect reduced motion. Pausing the training menu pauses the watchdog too.

The meadow runs a complete **3-minute day/night cycle**: warm sunrise, blue midday, golden sunset, then a moonlit night with stars. The sun follows a physically based solar path for 50°N on the equinox, so its orbit is tilted, it casts moving shadows, and day and night are each a minute and a half long. An **oversized stylized sun** with a layered corona floats in the sky, kept subtly alive by a slow breathing pulse; the night brings an equally huge **cratered moon** with dark maria, rim-lit craters and a silver-blue halo.

A small herd lives in the meadow: two sheep (Mallow and Poppy), a cow (Clover) and a stag (Bramble). Bramble and Poppy are hunters: they chase nearby zombies and headbutt them, casting the same elemental spells and wearing their own distinct cybernetic armor, neck guards and elemental crowns once trained. Mallow and Clover are builders, who anchor the defense economy by quarrying and stacking walls.

The builders choose flat sites with nearby natural quarry material, walk outside the clearing to gather it one block at a time, and carry the load between their horns. Each project is a straight, two-block-high wall section ten blocks long, with every block at least twelve blocks (24 world units) from the Hearth Tree, near the outer edge of the starting meadow. Sections run across the dominant incoming wave direction, using the closest voxel axis; between waves they face the radial approach to the Hearth. All herd NPCs gallop at the goat’s Shift speed, with Vector Striders and terrain effects applying once. Hunters and builders share movement, route planning, cave excavation and recovery. Every block is real and collides with the player and zombies. Builders revisit their completed sections and replace missing blocks before starting new ones, so walls damaged by zombies are repaired automatically. Builders quarry only on the side of their wall facing away from the Hearth, at least three block rows beyond the wall line. Both wall materials and tower stone follow this rule. Active and completed walls retain their foundations and two approach rows; a newly built player wall also invalidates a pending quarry hit beneath it.

Each spire costs **12 collected coins and 3 quarried stone**; builders uncover and mine natural stone outside the clearing, without using the player's wallet or hotbar. All builders can collect nearby dropped cannons and redeploy them without spending coins or materials, preserving the recovered type and tier. They carry one cannon at a time and keep it if placement is blocked. Untrained builders continue making walls and leave coins for the player. All NPC roles use the same body-aware movement planner: direct clearance checks, cached routes around Hearth roots, and bounded terrain search around walls and concave corners. Routes account for body width, headroom, one-block rises, safe drops and water. They stay committed through jumps and replan after construction, target changes or relocation. Impossible wander points inside solids are abandoned; hunters temporarily skip blocked targets and keep attacking reachable enemies. Stationary combat and idle time are not treated as movement stalls. A second progress check survives job changes and ignores jumping in place, and recovery deadlines continue through digging and climbing. Swimming NPCs prioritize a body-sized route to a reachable bank over their current land job; the goat and herd share the same bounded water search and validated shore jumps. Persistent failed escapes use a dry rescue point outside the pit rather than another point on its floor. In strategic view, distant goat coin runs do not relocate the working herd away from the Hearth. Herd routes use body-sized diagonal paths through voxel centres, so an off-centre start does not hide narrow passages. Animals recheck blocked route segments and abandon destinations on raised Hearth roots before starting recovery jumps. NPC recovery measures progress toward the current walk, hunt, quarry, build, climb, or descent target. A blocked animal tries a validated jump, then a local detour, then digs a body-sized opening and uses the recovered blocks to build steps out. Underground recovery also shares the goat’s bounded ascending excavation planner, preserving landing blocks and protected construction. Routes can descend safe drops of up to four blocks from roofs. Recovery can dismantle placed blocks when physically necessary; ordinary quarrying still leaves player construction alone, and the Hearth Tree foundation remains protected. Solvable escapes happen through normal movement and block edits, with relocation reserved for a final failed-recovery fallback. `scripts/test-animal-recovery.mjs` covers all four NPC bodies, wall and root detours, changing obstacles, combat, excavation, crowds and different update rates. Animal quarrying creates no competing item drops.

Goat and herd movement blend through planted-hoof stance, ankle articulation and lifted recovery, with breathing, weight shifts, turn lean, landing compression and timed attack wind-ups. Layered fleece, expressive faces, cloven hooves and secondary ear, tail and bell motion give each species a distinct silhouette. Builders strike at the moment their block edit happens, and hunters wind up before dealing damage. Every enemy has hierarchical joints: zombies shamble and reach, skeletons loosen their bone gait, spiders step on eight two-segment legs, and brutes carry their armor with heavy sway. The original **Iron Canopy** melodic theme is a 104-second, 152 BPM instrumental with a prominent lead melody, harmonized chorus, piano interlude, guitar chords and driving drums. The bundled **MP3** is the primary playback source, with **Ogg Vorbis** as a browser-selected fallback; the WAV is an editing master. Native HTML audio loads these relative paths directly under both `file://` and a GitHub Pages repository subpath. It uses no `fetch`, external audio service, or Web Audio media-element routing, which can silence local files because of cross-origin restrictions. Playback begins with a browser-permitted click or keypress and loops automatically. The Music button fades out and pauses the track, then resumes its position when switched back on. Pausing or hiding the page, entering caves, and defeat lower the volume without restarting the theme.

Sound effects are synthesized in-browser. Zombies have original raspy, vowel-shaped moans, hurt and death cries, dry bites and scraping steps. Herd species have distinct calls, footsteps and jump/landing sounds. Wood, stone, soil and foliage have distinct block break/place sounds. Hoofbeats combine woody resonances and ground grit; splashes include spray and rising bubbles. Each of the ten towers has its own launch and impact signature: Ballista timber snap, Mortar siege boom, Frost ice chinks and a descending crystal rattle, Ember fire crackle, Venom wet spit and rising poison bubbles, Storm electric crackle and thunder, Watchman metallic crack and delayed bolt-action latch, Earthshaker stone rubble, Arcane rising shimmer and a sharp cutting swoosh, and Sunspire sustained major chords. Rapid pickups climb a short pentatonic pattern. Changing hotbar slots, arriving and cleared waves, Hearth damage and defeat also have cues. World effects attenuate and lose high frequencies with distance, and pan relative to the camera; distant events are culled, and crowd sounds are rate limited. Hoof and combat impacts use short, dry transients rather than exaggerated bass slides.

Sound effects use a filtered, compressed Web Audio output with volume headroom and a bounded voice budget. Every effect fades in and ends at exact silence, and finished voices release their audio nodes. The mastered music plays through native media output with its own background volume, independently of the effects mute. Important cues briefly lower the actual music volume; overlapping cues preserve the in-flight level and recover automatically. Rapid music toggles preserve the fade level, and late or rejected playback requests cannot cancel a newer start.

## CI and hosting

The default suite (`node --test scripts/test-*.mjs`) stays within 30 seconds. Run `node --test scripts/check-enemy-balance-tests.mjs` for the longer wave-12 and wave-15 defense simulations; focused combat CI also includes them.

Every push and pull request runs the CI workflow: it checks every classic-script module with `node --check`, runs `node --test scripts/test-*.mjs`, builds the playable `_site/` artifact, verifies all local asset references, and smoke-tests the result over a local HTTP server. The water tests cover river continuity, generation-cache eviction, terrain edits, swimming and diving, bed/ceiling collisions, shore exits, frame-rate stability, held-key combinations and the underwater camera. The audio tests cover silent effect envelopes, protected mixing, bounded polyphony, positional attenuation, camera-relative stereo, crowd limits, independent effects mute, native music startup and resume, rejected and overlapping playback requests, fades, cave/pause/defeat volume, cue ducking, distinct tower/material signatures, and resource cleanup during long playback.

Pushes to `main` also run the Pages workflow, which rebuilds and checks the same artifact and deploys it with the official GitHub Pages Actions. The published game is served from **[https://ripopov.github.io/goat-craft/](https://ripopov.github.io/goat-craft/)**.

Extended checks also run in CI: `node --test scripts/check-enemy-progression-tests.mjs scripts/check-tower-effects-tests.mjs`. The default suite keeps combat, gate transitions, final-boss completion, rewards, collision and reduced-motion regressions.
